A little about me

I was born in Israel in 1997, and I'm currently living in the Boston area while studying at Harvard.1Still figuring out the whole Boston winter thing. Ask me again in February.

Somewhere along the way, I also went to law school, became a lawyer and a member of the Israel Bar, and ended up pursuing an LL.M. at Harvard Law School. These days, I'm researching a question I keep coming back to: what happens to judicial judgment when judges start relying on AI?

December 2023

The reservist bonus that ended on day 60

In December 2023, I reported that the IDF had stopped paying a special daily bonus to reservists who were not commanders after 60 days of service. The report sparked a public debate, with members of the Knesset from across the political spectrum opposing the decision, as a mako column described.

Things I'm curious about

A few questions I've been thinking about lately. I don't have good answers to all of them yet.3If you have thoughts on any of these, I'd genuinely love to hear them.

  • If AI could make better decisions than human judges, would we still have a reason to insist on having a human judge?

  • What happens when AI systems' memories become evidence in court? And how much can we trust what they "remember"?

  • If a robot powered by an open-weight AI model causes harm, who should be held responsible?

  • Does it make any sense to punish an AI agent? And if it doesn't, what does that tell us about why we punish humans?

  • Why does tort law draw such a sharp line between physical and emotional harm? Do legal systems in other cultures see that distinction differently?

  • How free are our choices, really? And what happens to our autonomy when algorithms can predict what we'll do?
